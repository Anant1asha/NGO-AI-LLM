### 2.1 Core Definitions & Structural Invariants

1. **Definition of Rational Number**:
   - *Formal Definition*: Any number that can be expressed in the form $\frac{p}{q}$, where $p$ and $q$ are integers and $q \neq 0$.
   - *Numerator & Denominator*: $p$ is the dividend integer (numerator), $q$ is the divisor integer (denominator).
   - *Inclusion Properties*:
     - Every Natural Number $n \in \mathbb{N}$ is a rational number ($\frac{n}{1}$).
     - Every Whole Number $w \in \mathbb{W}$ is a rational number ($\frac{w}{1}$).
     - Every Integer $z \in \mathbb{Z}$ is a rational number ($\frac{z}{1}$).
     - Zero is a rational number: $0 = \frac{0}{q}$ for any non-zero integer $q$ ($0 = \frac{0}{1} = \frac{0}{-5}$).
     - Every positive fraction is a rational number, but rational numbers extend to negative quotients ($-\frac{3}{5}$) and zero.
     - Decimals with terminating or repeating periods (e.g., $5.64 = \frac{564}{100}$, $4.40 = \frac{22}{5}$) and percentages ($63\% = \frac{63}{100}$) are rational numbers.
     - Perfect roots ($\sqrt{36} = 6 = \frac{6}{1}$, $\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2 = \frac{-2}{1}$) are rational numbers.
   - *Undefined Invariant (Division by Zero)*:
     - Division by zero is mathematically undefined. Therefore, expressions with $q = 0$ ($\frac{4}{0}$, $\frac{-7}{0}$) are NOT numbers and cannot belong to $\mathbb{Q}$.

2. **Standard Form of a Rational Number**:
   - A rational number $\frac{p}{q}$ is in *Standard Form* if and only if:
     1. The denominator $q$ is strictly positive ($q > 0$).
     2. $p$ and $q$ are co-prime, meaning $\gcd(|p|, q) = 1$.
   - *Sign Normalization Rule*: If the denominator is negative ($\frac{a}{-b}$), multiply numerator and denominator by $-1$: $\frac{a \times (-1)}{-b \times (-1)} = \frac{-a}{b}$.
   - *Reduction Methods*:
     - *Method 1 (Successive Simplification)*: Cancel common prime factors sequentially until co-prime.
     - *Method 2 (HCF Division)*: Compute $g = \gcd(|p|, |q|)$ and divide both by $g$: $\frac{p \div g}{q \div g}$.

3. **Equivalent Rational Numbers**:
   - Two rational numbers $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$ are equivalent ($\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$) if they represent the same magnitude and point on the number line.
   - *Scaling Law*: Multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by any non-zero integer $m \neq 0$ produces an equivalent fraction: $\frac{a \times m}{b \times m} = \frac{a}{b}$.
   - *Cross-Multiplication Test*: $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \iff a \cdot d = b \cdot c$.

4. **Absolute Value of a Rational Number**:
   - The non-negative magnitude of a rational number, stripped of its directional sign:
     $$|\frac{p}{q}| = \begin{cases} \frac{p}{q} & \text{if } \frac{p}{q} \ge 0 \\ -\frac{p}{q} & \text{if } \frac{p}{q} < 0 \end{cases}$$
   - *Triangle Inequality*: For any two rational numbers $x, y \in \mathbb{Q}$, $|x + y| \le |x| + |y|$. Equality holds if and only if $x$ and $y$ share the same sign (or at least one is zero).

### 2.2 Number Line Representation & Infinite Density

1. **Coordinate Geometry on the Real Line**:
   - The origin is point $0$ ($O$).
   - Positive rational numbers are mapped to the right of $0$; negative rational numbers to the left of $0$.
   - *Unit Interval Partitioning Protocol*:
     1. To plot $\frac{p}{q}$ in standard form ($q > 0$):
     2. Express improper fractions as mixed numbers: $\frac{p}{q} = k + \frac{r}{q}$ where $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $0 \le r < q$.
     3. Identify the bounding consecutive integers $k$ and $k+1$ (or $k-1$ for negative numbers).
     4. Subdivide the unit interval between $k$ and $k+1$ into exactly $q$ equal segments.
     5. Move $|r|$ subdivision steps from $k$ (rightward for positive, leftward for negative) to pinpoint the exact coordinate.

2. **Density Property (Betweenness of Rational Numbers)**:
   - Between any two distinct rational numbers $x < y$, there exists an infinite number of rational numbers: $|\mathbb{Q} \cap (x, y)| = \aleph_0$.
   - *Method 1 (Mean / Midpoint Method)*:
     $$m_1 = \frac{x + y}{2}, \quad x < m_1 < y$$
     This process can be repeated recursively to generate endless distinct rationals.
   - *Method 2 (Common Denominator / Rescaling Method)*:
     Given $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$, equalize denominators via LCM $L = \text{lcm}(b, d)$. If more intermediate numbers are needed than exist between scaled numerators, scale both fractions by $(n + 1)$:
     $$\frac{a \times (n+1)}{b \times (n+1)}, \quad \frac{c \times (n+1)}{d \times (n+1)}$$

#### Specialized Identities & Laws:
1. **Distributive Law of Multiplication over Addition**:
   $$a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$$
2. **Distributive Law of Multiplication over Subtraction**:
   $$a \times (b - c) = (a \times b) - (a \times c)$$
3. **Multiplication by Zero (Zero Absorption)**:
   $$x \times 0 = 0 \times x = 0$$
4. **Division Identities**:
   $$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{a}{b} = 1 \quad (a \neq 0), \qquad \frac{a}{b} \div \left(-\frac{a}{b}\right) = -1, \qquad \frac{a}{b} \div 1 = \frac{a}{b}, \qquad \frac{a}{b} \div (-1) = -\frac{a}{b}$$

## 6. L-Truth Zero-Spoiler Scaffolding & Misconception Audit

In compliance with the L-Truth Standard (`benchmarks/qa_ltruth_benchmark.js`) and `QuestionSchemaValidator`:
1. **Zero-Spoiler Guarantee**: No misconception explanation (`m` field) or progressive hint (`H1`–`H4`) contains answer leaks, completed numerical evaluations, or giveaway phrases (`is`, `giving`, `becomes`, `instead of`, `to get`, `yielding`, `result is`, `should be`).
2. **Pedagogical Diagnostic Depth**: Every incorrect distractor explicitly diagnoses the authentic mental model failure (e.g. integer subtraction sign inversion, dropping double negatives, adding denominators instead of finding LCM, confusing additive inverse with multiplicative reciprocal).
3. **4-Tier Scaffolding Hierarchy**:
   - **H1 (Hook / Attention)**: Directs the student's gaze to the operational focus or structural element without mathematics.
   - **H2 (Concept / Relationship)**: Articulates the underlying mathematical principle or invariant (e.g. why denominators represent equal unit parts).
   - **H3 (Strategy / Operational Rule)**: Provides the actionable procedural heuristic (e.g. find common denominator, multiply by reciprocal).
   - **H4 (Checkpoint / Self-Verification)**: Prompts the final execution check before submission without doing the arithmetic for the learner.
