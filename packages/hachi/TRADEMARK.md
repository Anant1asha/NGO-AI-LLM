# Trademark policy

**Apache-2.0 licenses the code. It does not license the name.**

Section 6 of the Apache License, Version 2.0 states that the license "does not grant
permission to use the trade names, trademarks, service marks, or product names of the
Licensor". That is deliberate, and it is the mechanism that keeps a fork from presenting
itself as the official project.

## What you may do without asking

- Use, modify, and redistribute Hachi under the terms of [LICENSE](LICENSE), including
  commercially.
- State, factually, that your product **is built on** Hachi or **uses** Hachi.
- Use the name in a way that is clearly referential: *"a Hachi-compatible agent"*,
  *"forks of Hachi"*, *"Hachi plugin"*.
- Keep the name in a fork that is plainly not the official project, e.g.
  *"Hachi — my personal fork"*, provided you make clear it is unofficial.

## What you may not do without written permission

- Use **Hachi** or the Hachi mark/logo as the name of your product, company, or service.
- Present a fork or derivative as the official Hachi, or as endorsed by Open Gigantic.
- Use the marks in a way that suggests sponsorship or affiliation that does not exist.
- Register a domain, package name, or trademark that is confusingly similar.

## The `hachi-og` package name

The published Python package is `hachi-og`. If you fork and publish your own
distribution, please use a different package name and command name so users can tell
the two apart. The bare name `hachi` is already used by an unrelated PyPI project.
